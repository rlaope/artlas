# Review an Atlas motion revision

Use this loop when feedback changes an approved scene's acting, mood or sound. Keep the accepted base identity and current request in the spec; the [skill](../SKILL.md) is the authority for the visual rules.

**Scope guard.** For diagnostic-only or skill-only requests, isolate comparison proofs outside the production project and change only evidence, procedures and gates. Hash current sources and media before and after; include library copies only when they are explicitly in scope. Set every QA/output path override before constructing a renderer or `Scene`, since initialization itself may write files. Isolated diagnostic renders are permitted with every output redirected; do not write or regenerate production assets or exports, sync vendored skills, replace or register an export, or claim the reported scene is fixed. A production-hash mismatch blocks completion until the unexpected write is resolved.

1. **Define the change.** Record the feedback, affected timestamps and what the viewer must perceive. Name the accepted source ID/hash, preserved character cues, text policy, frame rate and exact music-file window. Give each requested mood or story condition its own mandatory gate: technical integrity and an earlier aesthetic pass do not establish a newly requested outcome. For a passing reveal or moving subject, use the applicable gates in [subject light and moving layers](moving-subject-light.md). This is ready when each requested change has an observable acceptance condition.
2. **Make a short proof.** Render the affected passage with the final pixel grid, cadence and audio treatment. Include the motion peak and both transitions. Decode a phone-width view, neighbouring-frame burst and representative stills from that MP4. This is ready when the proof covers each changed beat rather than only its most attractive still.
3. **Review in a separate pass.** Give another reviewer the request, spec and exact proof file/hash. Review the axes below and record pass/fail for every applicable mood/story gate from step 1 against actual encoded phone playback and decoded phases. Scores may help prioritize revisions, but cannot average away a failed gate. Record audio measurements and whether listening occurred. Each failed gate needs a timestamp, visible defect and concrete next change. Reported crawling fine edges block acceptance even if other axes and cadence pass.
4. **Address the weakest affected beat within scope.** When a media revision is authorized, correct the identified issue and render that passage plus its transitions again. In diagnostic-only or skill-only mode, compare isolated controls, record the finding and update the procedure or gate without changing production. Reuse evidence only for unchanged regions or streams with matching source, mask, renderer-control and stream hashes relevant to that claim; a changed shared light/composite stage invalidates its affected checks. Report any remaining condition, reason and evidence instead of marking it passed.
5. **Verify the requested outcome.** Proof approval permits the full render, not final delivery. For a media revision, inspect the exact single final master/hash, complete decode, cadence, sound and delivery hashes; review start, intervals between proof passages and ending, plus the repaired boundaries and applicable mood/story gates in that final encode. For routine delivery, verify actual playback of the saved canonical MP4, its media/audio properties and SHA256; retain rebuild sources and clean up the same piece's obsolete local delivery files and work folders only after these checks pass. For explicitly requested library registration, verify byte-identical copies and library integrity. For explicitly requested dashboard work, also check real play, later seeking, end looping and mute restoration. Do not create/restore a dashboard or ask for its location during ordinary delivery. For diagnostic-only or skill-only work, verify the evidence and procedure changes independently, confirm production hashes are unchanged and commit only the authorized updates; do not imply that the current video passed or was repaired.

| Axis | What the reviewer must establish |
| --- | --- |
| Acting visibility | Head, breathing, hair or expression changes read in phone-width playback. Unique frame hashes alone are insufficient. |
| Character identity | Face, silhouette, outfit and identifying colours still match the accepted base. |
| Edge continuity | Neck, hat, hands, braids and revealed background stay connected without doubled edges, holes or warping. Smoke starts at the moving ember. |
| Temporal stability | Fine hair and clothing lines stay coherent through motion in native- and phone-width playback and immediate neighbouring frames. Compare raw/encoded output and a matched motion control; separate observed crawl from an unverified causal explanation. |
| Gloom and light | The chosen cold or warm mood persists; the dominant source and any requested secondary light reach their intended planes while the room stays dark and readable. |
| Audio clarity and distance | The requested passage begins at the intended moment. Dry/wet balance, C50/T30, vocal-envelope correlation, transient scan and loudness support audible distant music. Listening, when available, evaluates the perceived space; measurements alone are labelled as such. |

Keep a compact record: `feedback | timestamp | acceptance condition | revision | evidence | verdict`. Store source URLs, offsets, hashes and measurements; keep song files and copied lyrics out of this repository. Start the next review by opening the short encoded proof at phone width.
